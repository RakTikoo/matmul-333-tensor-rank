# Symmetric ansätze: the Γ39 norm-gap kill, the involution-pairing kill, and the C13 feasibility verdict

**Date:** 2026-11-24. Three scoped statements about
equivariant rank-22 ansätze — decompositions whose term set
is a union of orbits of a group fixing the multiplication
tensor T. Two are exclusions (one exact over F3, one by
counting over ℚ and F3); the third is a feasibility study
whose solver system is built and in flight, undecided.
Symmetric non-existence results for other groups over ℂ are
in the literature (Burichenko, arXiv:2211.03404 and
arXiv:2211.06485); nothing here borrows their scope.

## Item 25 — Singer Γ39 over F3: excluded exactly

**The claim, at its approved wording**

"One symmetric ansatz is excluded exactly. Let Γ39 be the order-39 group generated by a Singer cycle of order 13 in GL(3,3) and the Frobenius map, acting on rank-1 tensors over F3 by simultaneous conjugation (with the transposed action on the third leg that the stored form of the multiplication tensor requires). The Γ39-fixed subspace of the tensor space has dimension 31, but every sum of whole Γ39-orbits of rank-1 tensors lands in a fixed 17-dimensional subspace of it, and the multiplication tensor is not in that subspace. Therefore no Γ39-equivariant decomposition of ⟨3,3,3⟩ into 22 terms over F3 exists, for any orbit partition. The obstruction is arithmetic, not numerical: the group order is divisible by the characteristic, and the norm map's image is too small (a modular norm gap). Parameter counting alone would not have excluded this ansatz. This is a statement about this one ansatz over F3; it is not evidence about rank 22 in general, and symmetric non-existence results for other groups over ℂ are in the literature (Burichenko)."

The mechanism, in one line: when the characteristic divides
the group order, orbit sums land in the image of the norm
map, which can be a proper subspace of the fixed space —
here 17 of 31 dimensions — and T is outside it. The ansatz
dies before any solver runs, and parameter counting alone
(margins −3 / +18 depending on convention) would not have
killed it. The standing lesson, applied throughout the
census of `data/symmetry-census.md`: for characteristic-p
equivariant ansätze, run the reachability test before the
parameter count.

## Item 26 — involution pairing: excluded by counting

**The claim, at its approved wording**

"A second ansatz family is excluded by counting alone. If a rank-22 decomposition were invariant under an involution of the tensor's symmetry group, its terms would split into swapped pairs and fixed terms; the involution leaves 365 independent equations on the 729-entry tensor space, while every pairing pattern of 22 terms supplies at most 286 effective unknowns — short by at least 79 in all cases, over Q and over F3 alike. No involution-paired rank-22 decomposition exists. (An order-2 group halves the equations but barely compresses the terms; that asymmetry is the whole obstruction.)"

## Item 27 — the C13-only Singer ansatz: feasibility verdict BUILD

**The claim, at its approved wording**

"The C13-only residue of the Singer idea survives the tests that killed its parent. A rank-1 tensor fixed by the order-13 Singer cycle is exactly a triple of elements of GF(27) (one per factor, in the commutant field), so the nine fixed terms of the only viable partition (one 13-orbit plus nine fixed terms) contribute 63 effective unknowns; the full system has 88 effective unknowns against 93 equations — the nearest-to-square full rank-22 ansatz we have measured. Unlike the Γ39 case (Item 11), there is no norm gap: the orbit sums alone span the whole 93-dimensional fixed space and the multiplication tensor lies in their span. We record the honest counterweight in the same place: a discrete count of the ansatz's image falls short of the equation count (log₃ 80.46 against 93), so a solution would require the tensor to be special beyond linear reachability. The system is specified in full in our record; a solver run on it is in flight and undecided at the time of this release. Nothing here is evidence that a C13-equivariant scheme exists."

The contrast with Item 25 is the point: for the order-13
subgroup alone there is no norm gap (the norm image is the
whole fixed space), so the ansatz survives both paper tests
and earns a solver run — which is in flight and undecided at
the date of this release. The discrete counterweight
(shortfall 12.54 in the log₃ count) is recorded in the claim
itself; feasibility is not evidence of existence.
